2　Product Highlights & Technical Moat

2.1　The "No Centralized Business-Logic Server" Architectural Moat

szpL2E's core architectural claim is "no single trusted server": matching, authentication, fund custody, and other core business logic never run on a server that szpL2E operates and can unilaterally control — smart contracts and off-chain protocols replace the traditional backend API. Relative to traditional centralized EdTech platforms, this yields three structural advantages that are hard to replicate quickly:

  • Externalized regulatory and trust cost: custody and trade-matching responsibility is distributed to mature decentralized protocols like CoW Protocol and Wormhole, reducing the tail risk of a single platform suffering a centralized data breach or a regulatory shutdown order.
  • Lower operational complexity from single-source, multi-platform deployment: Tauri v2 compiles one Rust core to iOS, Android, Windows, macOS, and Wasm Web simultaneously — currently one of the few mature cross-platform frameworks covering both desktop and mobile from a single core.
  • Portable learning outcomes and assets: verifiable credentials (NFT／SBT) and résumé assets directly use the ERC-1155／ERC-721＋EIP-2981 royalty standard, so a user's learning outcomes are never locked to a single platform.

2.2　Anti-Cheat & Verification: The Educational-Credibility Moat

The biggest credibility risk in the Learn-to-Earn space is "gaming the system, not real learning." szpL2E's verification design includes spaced-repetition testing, typing／action-speed anomaly detection, and device／behavioral fingerprint matching, replacing simple attendance counts with three axes — progress, retention, and quality coefficients — and writing anti-cheat determinations to an on-chain, hash-timestamped record: auditable and tamper-evident.

2.3　The Token Economy's Deflationary Design

To avoid the classic failure mode of "token inflation, too many issued for anyone to want to learn," szpL2E uses a dynamic decaying reward curve pegged to current circulating supply, combined with fixed-ratio token burns across multiple spending scenarios — unlocking advanced content, minting credentials, entering lotteries — creating self-adjusting deflationary pressure. Corporate-sponsored task funds are pre-funded by the sponsor into a smart contract; the platform never advances funds or handles the flow of money.

2.4　Modular Design for Horizontal Expansion

szpDRX and szpERX are, technically, two applications of the same "encrypted content + on-chain proof of ownership + conditional decryption + automatic royalty split" asset-tization framework — expanding into other digital-content types and other learning formats doesn't require rebuilding the underlying asset infrastructure, sharply lowering the marginal cost of expansion.

6.4　Cost-Structure Highlights

Under the "no centralized business-logic server" design principle, szpL2E's cost structure differs from a traditional centralized EdTech platform's in two structural ways: first, there's no need to build a large self-hosted cloud server cluster — decentralized storage and RPC-node costs can scale elastically with usage and be externalized; second, customer-support and anomaly-monitoring costs shift, in the absence of a centralized backend, into a much more thorough in-app help system and on-chain status-tracking UX investment. The main cash outlays are engineering-team compensation, third-party security audit fees (estimated $80K–$250K), decentralized-outsourcing art and music community costs, and legal／compliance advisory fees.

9　Risk Factor Disclosures

Risks disclosed in the source text of the Technical Architecture Plan v1.1 and the Task Breakdown & Fishbone Plan v2.0 are compiled below for investors' careful review.

9.1　Technical & Execution Risk

  • Cross-chain bridge security risk: Wormhole itself suffered a roughly $320M security incident in 2022; a flaw in szpEX's dual-routing-decision logic could cause duplicate charges or routing conflicts for user assets.
  • No-persist private-key security boundary: the Wasm web version and native version must share the same Rust security logic — a poorly designed sandbox boundary could leak a plaintext private key via serialization; the web version inherently has a larger attack surface than native.
  • Layer-2 chain-selection risk: a foundational decision for the entire decentralized infrastructure — once chosen, every smart contract downstream is built on that chain, making rework extremely costly.
  • Anti-cheat module false-positive risk: the core reputational risk for an education product — too high a false-positive rate hurts real learners, too low a detection rate lets the entire token economy be drained by grinding abuse.
  • Token-economy extreme-scenario instability risk: if the dynamic-adjustment algorithm destabilizes under extreme conditions, the cost to correct it is higher than under a fixed-amount model.

9.2　Regulatory & Compliance Risk

Cross-chain bridging, IP/copyright trading, and education services — plus the future ERP and prediction-market modules — span securities law, copyright law, and financial regulation across multiple legal domains. We recommend consulting a lawyer experienced in crypto and securities regulation early for a compliance review; nothing in this document constitutes legal advice. The SZP token's legal characterization has not yet been confirmed by a formal legal opinion, a key prerequisite that must be clarified before this fundraising round.

9.3　The UX Cost of the "No Centralized Business-Logic Server" Architecture

A no-backend architecture means no traditional customer-support system and no centralized anomaly-monitoring facility; szpL2E needs a much more complete in-app on-chain transaction tracking and error-recovery flow. A lost seed phrase is an inherent, irreversible limitation of a non-custodial wallet that the company cannot recover on the user's behalf — a limitation that must be communicated honestly as user trust is built.

9.4　Market and Financial-Projection Uncertainty

Chapter 4's market data is drawn from multiple third-party research firms whose methodologies and coverage differ; Chapter 6's financial projections and Chapter 7's valuation analysis are both built on current-stage (pre-launch) scenario assumptions that have not yet been validated against actual market data. Actual results may differ materially from these projections; investors should treat these forward-looking figures as a decision-support framework, not a guarantee.
